# Data Sources & What's Made Up

IC Studio Boss mixes real data, real history and game-design inventions. This page says which is which, so nobody mistakes a game number for a fact.

**Legend:**
- ✅ **Official data**, used as published.
- 📅 **Real history**: real events and dates, written up for the game.
- 📊 **Approximation**: based on real reported figures, but rounded or simplified.
- 🎲 **Game design**: invented or estimated to make the game play well. **Not real data.**
- 🎭 **Fiction**: invented names and stories.

## ✅ Inflation: Consumer Price Index

| | |
|---|---|
| **Series** | CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items, **not seasonally adjusted** |
| **Series ID** | `CUUR0000SA0` |
| **Base period** | 1982–84 = 100 |
| **Publisher** | U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics |
| **Coverage** | January 1913 – September 2026 (monthly) |
| **Raw file** | [tools/data/cpi-u-nsa.tsv](tools/data/cpi-u-nsa.tsv) (as supplied, unchanged) |
| **Game file** | [scripts/data/real/cpi.js](scripts/data/real/cpi.js), generated by `node tools/build-cpi.js` |

**How it's used.** The game uses one value per calendar quarter: the average of that quarter's published months. All money in the game engine is stored in **today's dollars**, meaning the latest published quarter (2026 Q3). In a real-history game, every amount on screen is converted to the dollars of the quarter you're playing:

`dollars then = dollars today × CPI(that quarter) ÷ CPI(2026 Q3)`

**Missing month.** BLS did not publish **October 2025** (the 2025 lapse in appropriations). The game does not estimate it: 2025 Q4 averages November and December only. BLS did the same. Its published H2 2025 average (324.000) is exactly the average of the five months it did publish.

**After the data ends** (an endless game past 2026 Q3), the price level is **projected** 🎲. It grows at the average quarterly rate of the previous ten years of real CPI. The money bar marks projected years with an asterisk (*).

**How the data was checked:**
- **Annual averages:** recomputed from the months, they match the BLS-published averages for 1950, 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020 and 2024 (the build script checks these on every run).
- **Half-year averages:** all 85 BLS-published half-year averages from 1984 to 2026 match the monthly data to the last published digit. Any ±0.001 differences come from BLS averaging its unrounded internal values.
- **Large monthly jumps:** every month-to-month change over 2.5% lines up with a known historical event: wartime inflation (1917–20), the 1921 deflation, July 1933, and the end of wartime price controls in July 1946.

## 📅 Real history: world and industry events

File: [scripts/data/real/history.js](scripts/data/real/history.js)

In a real-history game (pick **Era → Real history** and a start year from 1930 to 2026), each quarter is a real calendar quarter. Events take effect on their real dates. Examples:
- the Production Code (1934–68)
- World War II
- the arrival of television
- the Writers Guild strikes (1960, 1988, 2007–08, 2023)
- the Screen Actors Guild strikes (1960, 2023)
- *Jaws* and the summer blockbuster (1975)
- *Star Wars* (1977)
- the slasher boom
- the VHS and DVD eras
- 9/11
- the financial crisis
- the superhero era
- the COVID-19 shutdown

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| Event **names, dates and descriptions** | 📅 Real history, written from general historical knowledge. Dates are given to the quarter. An event that began late in a quarter may take effect the following quarter. |
| Event **effect sizes** (e.g. "War films +20%", "Horror +30%") | 🎲 **Game design.** They reflect the *direction* and rough strength of each event's impact on the movie business. They are **not** measured statistics. |
| **2020–2024 box office** multipliers | 📊 Approximation from reported North American annual box office relative to 2019 (about $11.4B): 2020 ≈ 0.19, 2021 ≈ 0.39, 2022 ≈ 0.65, 2023 ≈ 0.78, 2024 ≈ 0.75. How 2020 and 2021 are split across quarters is 🎲 game design (each year averages to its ratio). |
| **2025 onward** box office | 🎲 Assumed to stay near the 2024 level. No verified full-year figure is included. |
| **Loan interest by era** | 📊 Approximation of the U.S. bank prime rate in each era (e.g. 1.5% in 1934–47, ~20% at the 1980–81 peak, 3.25% in 2009–15). Periods with no entry use the base rate of about 8% a year. |
| **Library income** (home video / TV / streaming) | 🎲 Game design. The eras are real (VHS from the early 1980s, DVD from March 1997, streaming from the 2010s), but the dollar amounts are estimates. |
| Mapping real film types to the game's seven genres | 🎲 Game design. War films and westerns count as Action, musicals as Family. |

After the history data ends (2026 Q3), the game switches to random industry news (🎭).

## 🎭 Fiction

- **All people are fictional:** actors, directors and writers, with names randomly combined from first-name and surname lists. So are studios, film titles and loglines.
- **Iowa City locations** (Old Capitol, Kinnick Stadium, Ped Mall…) are real places, but their filming fees, genre fits and "draw" are 🎲 invented.
- **The Golden Cob Awards** and the **Iowa City Film Festival** event are fictional.
- **Random industry news** (genre crazes, tax credits…) is fictional. In real-history games, only era-neutral items are used alongside the real events.
- **Scandal cards** are fictional and only ever involve fictional people.

**Real people.** The game does not use real actors, directors or writers. If real talent is ever added, scandal cards will be switched off for them automatically. The game will never put a real person in an invented scandal.

## Updating the data

- **New CPI months:** paste the BLS table into [tools/data/cpi-u-nsa.tsv](tools/data/cpi-u-nsa.tsv) and run `node tools/build-cpi.js`. It re-checks the annual averages and regenerates `scripts/data/real/cpi.js`.
- **History:** edit [scripts/data/real/history.js](scripts/data/real/history.js) and update `HISTORY_END`. Keep the 📅 vs 🎲 split clear in comments.
